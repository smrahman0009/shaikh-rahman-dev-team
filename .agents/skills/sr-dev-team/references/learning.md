# Reviewed learning

Persisted lessons improve future working context; they do not retrain model weights.

Keep separate scopes: user preferences, reusable team practices, specialist lessons, project-specific decisions, and temporary task state. Default new lessons to the narrowest supported scope. A project technology choice is not automatically a global rule.

After a meaningful failure or correction, propose a compact lesson:

- Problem and observed consequence.
- Evidence-supported cause.
- Corrective action and demonstrated result.
- Applicability and exclusions.
- Prevention or detection method.
- Source task/evidence, review identity, date, and status.

A separate critic checks factual support, scope, duplication, conflict with existing lessons, and whether a regression check would be more effective. Promote only validated lessons. Retire or supersede stale lessons with a reason; retain provenance. Conflicts remain explicit until resolved.

Retrieve by current task/domain and use only applicable approved lessons. Do not load the whole history. Do not export private project artifacts, identifiers, credentials, or business details into another project's context. Cross-project promotion requires a sanitized, general lesson and authorized storage. Do not automatically write outside the workspace.

Maintain knowledge after meaningful work, not after every tool call. Track repeated mistakes and practical improvements when measurable. More memory is not evidence of learning.
